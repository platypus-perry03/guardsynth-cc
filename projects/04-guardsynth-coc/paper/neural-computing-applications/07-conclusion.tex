\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Compliance with externally specified behavioral conditions remains a reliability
concern even for driving models that explain their decisions. We addressed this
by including behavioral constraints in the training data of CoC-based driving
models and proposed GuardSynth. GuardSynth binds each constraint field to an
observation, a supplied rule, or a modeling assumption, represents the constraint
as an EBLC specification, checks it within a restricted logical model, and
appends CNL generated from the specification to the original CoC.

Training with constraint sentences improved rule-dependent selection. We
compared adapters with identical initialization and common CNL evaluation
inputs, restricting this comparison to conditions trained with cross-entropy
loss alone. Adapters trained with constraint sentences had violation rates of
at most 1.56\% and rule-pair accuracy of at least 86.98\%. Adapters trained on
CoC alone had violation rates of 6.25--17.92\% and rule-pair accuracy of at most
56.77\%, despite receiving the same rules at evaluation. Neither training with
semantic loss without rule text nor providing the same rule to an untrained
Qwen3-VL-8B model attained the rule-pair accuracy of adapters trained with
constraint text; the 8B model violated the rule in 68.75\% of pedestrian-entry
predictions. Across six
behavioral tasks, both natural-language constraints and EBLC-derived CNL
improved rule-dependent selection, while shuffling case--constraint associations
substantially reduced the gains. These findings support including case-specific
constraints in training data as well as providing them at inference.

GuardSynth adds checkability to training constraints. In fault-injection
evaluation of the two specifications used for learning, the checking suite
detected all 288 supported faults and accepted all 48 valid cases.
Specification-level checks detected faults in the specification and logical
formula, and only sentence correspondence detected faults in the generated
CNL. The 48 cases in which evidence records
and specifications were jointly wrong remained undetected, indicating that
evidence validity requires independent confirmation beyond internal consistency
checks. This evaluation, conducted after the training runs, assessed the
checkability of the specifications; it does not account for the reported
behavioral improvement.

The training findings come from synthetic candidate-selection tasks, one 2B
model family, and three optimization seeds. Performance declined under
rewording, and violations persisted in the earlier XY diagnostic, whose prohibited
candidates exceed the radius by as little as 1~mm. Future work will
extend this path to assessing constraint applicability in real scenes,
constraint-based training of trajectory-generating VLA models, and
deployment-time constraint generation. GuardSynth provides a path for including
case-specific constraints in training data and checking their construction and
correspondence, enabling constraint-based learning to be developed in a
checkable manner.
