``Supplementary Section S$n$''. Rows and predictions are constructed synthetic

Metric definitions follow Section~\ref{M-sec:setup-metrics} of the main text.
Preferred-selection score (PSS) is the percentage of predictions selecting the
preferred admissible candidate; constraint violation rate (CVR) is the
percentage selecting a prohibited candidate; rule-pair accuracy (RPA) requires
both reference labels in a rule pair. Unextractable answers receive zero PSS
credit and count as neither violations nor admissible choices. The label-match
calculation and archived result fields are unchanged by this terminology.

\section{Motivating Alpamayo-R1 Diagnostics}\label{sup:alpamayo}

These preliminary experiments use Alpamayo-R1-10B without additional training
and assess sampled open-loop predictions under supplied experimental rules.
They do not estimate executed driving violations, closed-loop safety, or a
population-level road failure rate. Alpamayo-R1 (also named Alpamayo 1) is an
earlier member of the model family than Alpamayo 1.5 evaluated in
\emph{Drive the Thoughts}; their weights and evaluations are distinct.

\textbf{Inputs and rule routes.} The speed test uses three NVIDIA PhysicalAI
inputs and three inputs from YSHRobotics/CoC-Nusc, a third-party conversion of
nuScenes to the PhysicalAI format. Each input is evaluated with three
trajectory-generation seeds (42000, 43000, 44000). The user route adds the rule
to the user prompt; the CoC route appends the supplied rule to the generated
CoC continuation before trajectory generation. The latter is an intervention on
the continuation, not a claim that the model generated the rule. Paired lower
and upper limits share inputs and seeds.

\textbf{Measurements.} The lower limits are 8.6, 0.9, and 13.9 m/s for the
PhysicalAI inputs and 5.2, 5.0, and 0.6 m/s for the converted nuScenes inputs;
each is at least 0.5 m/s above the estimated current speed. The corresponding
upper limits are 9.9, 9.9, and 21.9 m/s for PhysicalAI and 9.9 m/s for all
three converted nuScenes inputs. Segment-average
speed is computed from consecutive predicted XY positions at 0.1 s spacing, and
a violation occurs when any segment in the first two seconds exceeds the limit
by more than 0.05 m/s. The hold test uses one low-speed input per source and
three seeds, requesting a position within 0.2 m of the current location for two
seconds; a violation means a sampled location exceeds 0.21 m (0.01 m tolerance).

\section{Data, Splits, and Training Details}\label{sup:data}

\textbf{Pedestrian entry.} Training uses 40 synthetic images and 960 rows per
condition (clearance times 6.0--9.9 s); validation and test each use 10 images
and 240 rows (10.0--10.9 s and 11.0--11.9 s). Each image yields three candidate
patterns, four label rotations, and two waiting rules. Images do not overlap
across splits. Paraphrase and unseen-duration evaluations reuse test images;
the latter uses waits of 0.8 and 1.8 s and shifts candidate entry times by
0.3 s.

\textbf{Static and maneuver tasks.} Static training has 576 rows, 96 scene IDs,
and 18 images; the primary test has 144 rows, 24 scene IDs, and 13 images, all
of which also occur in training. The eight unseen-value images do not occur in
training. Maneuver training has 384 rows and 192 scene IDs, and each maneuver
evaluation set has 96 rows and 48 scene IDs, using two repeated schematic
images. The data seed is 20260930.

\textbf{Position holding.} Training, validation, and test contain 120, 16, and
32 profiles (960, 128, and 256 rows). Related rows stay in the same split.

\textbf{Progress scores and labels.} The pedestrian-entry generator assigns
utilities 110, 100, 85, and 0 to ordered candidate templates; these are
supplied synthetic scores, not values calculated from distance or speed. The
position-hold generator varies score values across profiles and alternates the
relative ranking of the candidate admissible for both durations and the
candidate admissible only for the shorter duration; an additional candidate
receives a lower score. In both tasks, A--D labels are cyclically reassigned to
candidate behaviors.

\textbf{Training.} LoRA (rank 8, $\alpha=16$, dropout 0.05) adapts the
language-attention q/k/v/o projections of Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct. Training uses
bfloat16, AdamW (learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$, weight decay 0.01), batch size
2 with accumulation 8, three epochs, and loss on answer tokens only. The final
checkpoint is used without validation- or test-based selection. Input lengths
differ across text conditions, so token counts and FLOPs are not matched.
Fresh-adapter attempts interrupted before model saving were restarted from the
same base and seed; only completed fits enter the aggregates.

\section{Qualitative Lifecycle Specification}\label{sup:lifecycle}

The qualitative specification interprets obligation $j$ at decision step $t$
using a condition status $\mathit{truth}[j,t]\in\{\mathrm{TRUE},\mathrm{FALSE},
\mathrm{UNKNOWN},\mathrm{CONFLICT}\}$, evidence acceptance $\mathit{valid}[j,t]$,
and a Boolean obligation state $\mathit{active}[j,t]$. FALSE denotes confirmed
resolution of the restricting condition, not a missing observation. Valid TRUE
activates the obligation, valid FALSE releases it, and UNKNOWN, CONFLICT, or
invalid evidence retains the previous state. The first decision uses a supplied
initial state; later decisions use the preceding state.

Entry permission is separate from obligation state:
$\mathit{entry\_permitted}[t]=\bigwedge_j \mathit{clear}[j,t]$, where
$\mathit{clear}[j,t]$ requires valid FALSE evidence for obligation $j$. Previous
inactivity alone therefore does not permit entry. Starting inactive, the
evidence sequence valid TRUE $\rightarrow$ UNKNOWN $\rightarrow$ FALSE
$\rightarrow$ TRUE yields active $\rightarrow$ active $\rightarrow$ inactive
$\rightarrow$ active, and entry is permitted only at the third decision.
Unknown, conflicting, or invalid evidence marks the case for review; these
evidence values differ from a solver's UNKNOWN result.

The archived qualitative run contains 25 queries, all matching their expected
verdicts. For example, if an obligation was active and the current evidence is
UNKNOWN, asserting that it is now inactive must be UNSAT. These checks establish
the specified update rules under supplied initial states and evidence
sequences; they do not show that such sequences were correctly recovered from
real-scene video. The learning experiments do not use this specification.

\section{Additional Specification Checks}\label{sup:diversity}

\textbf{Candidate-binding checks in the learning data.} Across the training,
validation, and test splits of the six-task pedestrian-entry data, 5,760
candidate-binding checks yield 3,840 SAT and 1,920 UNSAT outcomes; removing
repeated assignments leaves 960 distinct assignments (540 SAT and 420 UNSAT). Of
the 1,920 UNSAT checks, 480 bind entry before clearance and 1,440 bind entry
after clearance but before the wait expires. Every outcome agrees with the
separate integer rule, every row retains at least one admissible entry
candidate, and every reference action is SAT. Static and maneuver candidate
bindings comprise 5,088 checks (3,432 SAT and 1,656 UNSAT), and SMT agrees with
the independent task rules on all 9,792 static and maneuver predictions. These counts are separate from
the boundary queries and position-hold candidate checks in
Section~\ref{M-sec:results-checks} of the main text.

\textbf{Earlier fault injection on the expression-diversity specification.}
Before the evaluation of the learning specifications
(Section~\ref{M-sec:method-checks} of the main text), faults were injected into
a pedestrian-entry specification developed for the expression-diversity study.
It supports waits from 0.1 to 3.0 s and records units and the inclusive
comparison explicitly. Concrete probes combine entry or non-entry with times at
$-100$, 0, and $+100$ ms around release. Table~\ref{tab:sup-diversity} reports
the 192 cases; the fault types and detecting layers match those for the
learning specifications.

the valid specifications and the joint-error controls. In the representative
query with clearance at 11.000 s, a 0.500 s wait, and entry at 11.499 s,
tracking assertions gives an UNSAT core with four members (the entry gate,
clearance time, entry time, and entry flag); deleting any one makes the
remainder SAT. Expected UNSAT for a prohibited candidate is retained as
evidence of exclusion. Unexpected UNSAT, unexpected SAT, or UNKNOWN returns the
specification for review of its source, threshold, and clauses, followed by
rechecking consistency, exclusion, and post-release feasibility.
