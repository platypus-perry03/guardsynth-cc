# GuardSynth: Augmenting Chain-of-Causation with Verified Behavioral Constraints for Autonomous Driving

## Abstract

Driving models increasingly explain their actions in natural language, but a convincing explanation does not establish rule compliance. A recent study of Alpamayo 1.5 found that one third of its reasoning traces were unreliable, and trajectories agreed with reliable reasoning in only 74% of cases. Even agreement does not ensure compliance with an external speed limit or waiting rule. Chain of Causation (CoC) explains why a decision was made, but may leave the conditions defining allowed actions implicit. Our preliminary Alpamayo tests found violations despite explicit inference-time rules. These observations motivate explicit training constraints and checks on their correctness. We present GuardSynth, a method that records the sources of constraints, represents them in Evidence-Bound Lifecycle Contracts (EBLC), checks supported logical properties, and generates Controlled Natural Language (CNL) sentences appended to CoC. With identical evaluation rules, additional position-hold training yields 22/192, 1/192, and 0/192 violations for CoC-only, natural-language-constraint, and EBLC-derived-CNL training, respectively, on the 2 s interval-maximum test. Across six synthetic action-selection tasks, both natural-language constraints and EBLC-derived CNL improved accuracy and reduced violations relative to CoC alone. With constraints supplied during training and evaluation, temporal-task accuracy increased from 49.86% to over 96%, while violations fell from 19.17% to below 1.5%. Specification checks detected all 144 injected errors across six supported types and accepted all 24 valid contracts, but missed 24 errors in which evidence and specification were wrong together.
