# VIII. Conclusion

GuardSynth provides a systematic development path for behavioral constraints that augment Chain of Causation. It records supplied constraint content and its sources, binds it to supported EBLC fields and semantics, checks a restricted logical representation, and generates CNL for insertion after the original CoC. The task–field–formula–CNL–verdict mapping connects this method to executable examples and evaluation.

With identical evaluation rules, the primary position-hold study ends with 22/192, 1/192, and 0/192 strict violations for CoC-only, natural-language-constraint, and EBLC-derived-CNL training, respectively, on the 2.0 s interval-maximum test. Residual XY diagnostic failures show that this improvement depends on the tested representation and numerical distribution.

Across six synthetic candidate-selection tasks, our existing natural-language constraint path and the EBLC-derived CNL path achieve higher primary accuracy and lower violation rates than CoC alone. Separate error and boundary analyses demonstrate supported checks while exposing shared-source errors and residual model violations. The contribution combines the behavioral utility of explicit constraints with a traceable formal development interface; broader real-scene applicability and generalization remain future work.
